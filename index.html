Course organization and learning modules

KES 1.4

Chunk your content by week, unit, or topic so students can see how the course is laid out and where they are in it.

In the old Blackboard Learn, folders were the highest-level organizer. In Blackboard Ultra, learning modules are available as the top level, and I recommend them. They bring more tools than folders, including the ability to add a module image.

Recommendation, not requirement. You do not have to use the learning module tool. What matters is that your content is clearly chunked.

Naming your modules

Name them whatever you like: Module 1, Week 1, Unit 1, Section 1.

In my own courses, I avoid time-based names like "Week." When a course moves from 16 weeks to 8, I only need to revise the calendar and due dates, not rename every module.

Module overviews

KES 1.1, 1.8, and 1.9

Give each module a dedicated space for three things: the learning objectives, why the content matters, and instructions for completing the work.

A simple outline you can reuse

  • What you'll learn: the learning objectives for the module (KES 1.1)
  • Why it matters: how this content connects to the course, the program, or the job (KES 1.8)
  • What to do: the steps for working through the module (KES 1.9)
  • What to submit: the graded work and when it is due (KES 1.9)

Accessibility: PDFs, Word documents, and images

The goal: an Ally score of 85% or higher, with no red items.

Where to look: in your Blackboard course, open Books & Tools in the right-hand menu. The Ally Accessibility Report is at the top of the list.

Common issues and their fixes
Common issueFix
Scanned PDFRun OCR (text recognition) so the page contains real text instead of a picture of text.
Document with no structureUse the built-in styles (Heading 1, Heading 2, and so on) instead of bolding or enlarging text by hand.
Table with no headersIdentify the header row in the table's settings.
Image with no descriptionAdd meaningful alt text that says what the image shows and why it is there.

Accessibility: PowerPoint and AI-generated decks

  • Use the built-in slide layouts and placeholders rather than free-floating text boxes.
  • Add alt text to informative images.
  • Mark purely decorative objects as decorative.
  • Check the reading order on each slide.

Attractive slides still need an accessibility review. This includes decks made with AI tools. A deck can look polished and still be unreadable to a screen reader.

Rubrics and assessment grading

KES 3.1

Tell students how they earn points on discussions, essays, reflections, and projects.

A rubric can be a short statement or a checklist. It does not need to be a large grid, and it does not need to be built into Blackboard.

Example

A full-credit response includes:

  • An original post that answers every part of the prompt
  • At least one specific reference to this week's reading
  • Replies to two classmates by the due date

Participation and outside activities

KES 2.7

If your course requires observation hours, community service, or a similar activity, explain three things:

  • The requirements: what students must do and by when
  • The documentation: how students show they completed it
  • The impact on the grade: how it counts

Student feedback throughout the course

Give students a way to tell you how the course is going while it is still running. Two easy options:

  • An open feedback discussion forum
  • A short student feedback survey

A midterm reflection is another option.

A useful Start Here module

A Start Here module brings together what students need before the coursework begins:

  • Instructor contact information
  • Syllabus
  • Course policies
  • Netiquette
  • Blackboard tutorials
  • Technology help
  • OCTC support resources

I am developing Start Here resources that faculty can use in their own courses. Ask me what is available.

Course technology instructions and support

KES 4.17

For each third-party platform your course uses, I recommend a clearly labeled module that contains:

  • Access instructions
  • Tutorials
  • Support links
  • Guidance about where students complete their work and where they submit it
